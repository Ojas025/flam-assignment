# B3 — The misread column in REPORT_v0 Section 2

## The column

Both conclusions in REPORT_v0 Section 2 rest on the same misreading of one
column: `reported_tok_s`. The harness defines it by the identity

    reported_tok_s = num_requests × (prompt_len + gen_len) / wall_clock_s

It therefore counts prefill (prompt) tokens alongside output tokens. For
generated-token throughput — the figure users actually experience — divide
by `(prompt_len + gen_len) / gen_len`, or compute
`gen_tokens / wall_clock_s` directly.

## The two claims

| REPORT_v0 claim | What the log shows |
|---|---|
| "Long prompts give better GPU utilization (1311 vs 883 tok/s at batch 16)" | The reported figure is higher only because each long request contributes 4096 counted tokens, against 768 for a short one. Goodput at batch 16: long = 16 × 512 / 49.97 = **163.9 tok/s**; short = 16 × 256 / 13.91 = **294.5 tok/s**. Short prompts are **1.8×** better. |
| "Assume ~1600 tok/s per L4 and scale linearly, so batch 48 gives ~3200 tok/s" | The 1600 is the batch-24 reported figure — i.e. **~201 tok/s** of real output. It is also the peak immediately before the KV-cache wall; beyond it, throughput falls (B2). Batch 48 measured: **1299** reported, **162** goodput. The forecast of 3200 is **~20×** the real number. |

## What the report should have said

- Short prompts out-perform long prompts on generated-token throughput: **294 vs 164 tok/s at batch 16**.
- Capacity planning: one L4 peaks at about **200 generated tok/s** on 3.5k-token prompts (batch 24), limited by KV-cache capacity of **~25 full-length sequences**.
- Beyond batch ~25, throughput declines because of preemption and recompute. **Do not extrapolate linearly.**
- Do not advise clients to pack more context per request: it reduces output throughput and consumes KV capacity.
