# B2. Throughput anomaly in the long-context sweep (prompt 3584, gen 512)

## The anomaly

| n | wall (s) | reported tok/s | goodput (gen tok/s) | kv_cache_util | preempted_seqs | itl_ms_p50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 28.98 | 565.4 | 70.7 | 0.16 | 0 | 51.33 |
| 8 | 36.30 | 902.6 | 112.8 | 0.31 | 0 | 62.26 |
| 16 | 49.97 | 1311.4 | 163.9 | 0.62 | 0 | 77.20 |
| **24** | 61.16 | **1607.4** | **200.9** | 0.93 | 0 | 96.07 |
| 32 | 94.71 | 1384.0 | 173.0 | 0.97 | 7 | 101.79 |
| 48 | 151.41 | 1298.5 | 162.3 | 0.97 | 23 | 100.00 |

Throughput rises 184% from n=4 to n=24, then **falls 13.9% at n=32 and 19.2% at n=48**, although concurrency rose 33% and 100% over the n=24 row. "Throughput scales with batch" predicts a continued rise.

Prompt and generation lengths are constant across the sweep, so goodput = n × 512 / wall_clock = reported_tok_s / 8 (200.9 → 173.0 → 162.3 tok/s). The reversal appears in both metrics, so it is not an artifact of how the harness counts tokens. The identity n × 4096 / wall_clock = reported_tok_s holds on every row to within rounding (≤0.11).

## Mechanism: KV-cache exhaustion, preemption, recompute

1. **Demand exceeds capacity.** B1 gives ~25.7 full-length (4096-token) sequences. n=32 and n=48 ask for 1.24× and 1.87× the KV budget. `kv_cache_util` rises 0.62 → 0.93 at n=24 and is pinned at 0.97 for both larger runs.
2. **The scheduler evicts.** `preempted_seqs` goes 0 → 7 → 23, consistent with the overflow above 25 (32 − 25 = 7, 48 − 25 = 23). This is supporting evidence, not proof: the counter is "sequences preempted at least once", and two data points are few. vLLM's default preemption mode is recompute, so an evicted sequence loses its KV and redoes its prefill.
3. **The lost time is not in decode.** Decode time is about (gen_len − 1) × ITL; the rest of the wall clock is prefill, recompute and waiting:

| n | decode = 511 × ITL (s) | wall (s) | non-decode = wall − decode (s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 39.4 | 49.97 | 10.5 |
| 24 | 49.1 | 61.16 | 12.1 |
| 32 | 52.0 | 94.71 | **42.7** |
| 48 | 51.1 | 151.41 | **100.3** |

   Decode stays at ~50 s, and `itl_ms_p50` stays within 6% of its n=24 value (96.07 → 101.79 → 100.00 ms). Non-decode time rises 12.1 → 42.7 → 100.3 s. ITL is flat because only ~25 sequences are resident at once; the extra requests wait or are recomputed instead of decoding.
4. **Control.** The short-prompt sweep (512 + 256) on the same GPU reaches n=64 at `kv_cache_util` 0.47 with 0 preemptions, and throughput is still rising (2267.3 tok/s). Batch size alone does not reverse throughput. (The two sweeps differ in prompt length, so this is supporting evidence, not a clean experiment.)
5. **TTFT is weak evidence here.** `ttft_ms_p50` rises 500.5 → 636.9 → 955.4 ms (+91% from n=24 to n=48), which is consistent with queueing and re-prefill contention. But recompute happens after a sequence's first token, so it is not a direct cause of TTFT. And the absolute values look implausibly low: 24 simultaneous 3584-token prefills is ~722 TFLOP of compute, about 6–12 s on an L4, not ~0.5 s. I rely on wall clock, ITL and the KV columns instead.

## Proposed change: fp8 KV cache (`--kv-cache-dtype fp8`)

```
bytes/token = 2 × 28 × 8 × 128 × 1 = 57,344 B (56 KiB)
capacity    = 12.08e9 / (57,344 × 4096) = 51.4  →  51 sequences (was 25)
util @ n=48 = 48 × 57,344 × 4096 / 12.08e9 = 0.933   (same headroom n=24 has today)
util @ n=32 = 0.622
```

n=48 now fits with no preemption, so no prefill is redone.

**Predicted effect** (measured fp16 → predicted fp8):

| | n=32 | n=48 |
|---|---|---|
| preempted_seqs | 7 → 0 | 23 → 0 |
| kv_cache_util | 0.97 → 0.62 | 0.97 → 0.93 |
| reported tok/s, **floor** | 1384 → ≥1607 (+16%) | 1298 → ≥1607 (+24%) |
| reported tok/s, **central estimate** | → ~2,340 | → **~2,690** |
| goodput, central estimate | 173 → ~293 | 162 → **~336** |
| wall clock, central estimate | 94.7 → ~56 s | 151.4 → **~73 s** |

- **Floor.** The no-preemption rows rise monotonically (565 → 903 → 1311 → 1607), so with every sequence resident, throughput should be at least the n=24 value unless a new bottleneck appears. This is an assumption.
- **Central estimate.** Decode step ≈ (8.4 GB weights + resident KV bytes) / (300 GB/s × 0.66), where 0.66 is the efficiency I fitted on the 11 non-preempted rows (observed ITL ≈ model / 0.64–0.67). At n=48 with fp8 KV: (8.4 + 10.6) GB / 198 GB/s ≈ 96 ms, so decode ≈ 49 s. Non-decode time ≈ 12.1 s × 48/24 = 24 s, assuming prefill scales linearly (12.1 s at n=24 matches the ~6–12 s prefill compute estimate above). Total ≈ 73 s.
- **Not predicted:** TTFT, because the logged TTFT values are not trustworthy (point 5).

**Risks.** fp8 KV can reduce long-context quality and needs an eval before adoption. Kernel support for this model on the L4 is unverified. The central estimate is a model fitted to this log, not a measurement.

**Alternative (config only): `max_num_seqs=24`.** It removes preemption at no quality cost, but it queues the excess and runs it as a second wave. n=48 becomes two full waves: 2 × 61.16 = 122 s, 1607 tok/s reported (+24%). n=32 becomes 24 + 8: 61.16 + 36.30 = 97.5 s, **~1345 tok/s (−3% vs 1384)**, so no gain. Queued requests also wait a full wave, so their TTFT rises sharply. It only helps when the request count is close to a multiple of 24.

## Confirming the mechanism
Pull `vllm:num_preemptions_total`. It should be 0 for n ≤ 24 and ≥7 and ≥23 at n=32 and n=48, and 0 everywhere after the fp8 change.
