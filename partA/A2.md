## Method findings

- **E3 — averaging:** macro vs micro differ by ≤ **2.06%** (sample) and ≤ **1.24%** (FLORES). Tokens/sentence match under both. Use macro for “typical sentence,” micro for total cost.
- **E4 — which unit:** the same Hindi text reads **0.46×–10.65×** English depending on unit and tokenizer. For serving cost across languages, use **tokens per parallel sentence**. Tokens per character are within-language only.
- **E7 — “two metrics agree”:** invalid. Both ratios share the same token count. On the same text, five units span **2.82×–11.11×**.

## What actually moves the numbers

- **E6 — tokenizer, not script:** on FLORES, Hindi/English fertility is **6.10×** (gpt2), **0.97×** (muril), **0.96×** (sarvam-1). Dravidian ratios fall from **16.07–21.39×** (gpt2) to **1.35–1.69×** (muril) and **1.46–1.94×** (sarvam-1) — a **90–93%** drop. On the sample, Hindi/English goes **5.89× → 1.00×**. English has *more* characters per word than Hindi (**5.97** vs **5.10**), so v0’s “script property” claim fails.
- **E7 — routing:** per sentence, Hindi costs **4.82×** English on gpt2 but **0.84×** on muril.
- Budgeting **6×** on top of an Indic model double-counts: Hindi is already **1.0–1.16×** English on that model → **417–500%** over-provisioning.
