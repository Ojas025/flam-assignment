# A2. Experiment summary

Seven ablations (E1–E7, `NOTEBOOK.md`) against the v0 `fertility.py`, each
changing one variable. Full logs live in the notebook; this is what they showed.

## What was wrong with v0

| Bug | Impact | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| `split(" ")` created empty words (E1) | Fertility understated, language-dependent — up to +3.60% on Kannada (30% of lines) vs 0.00% English | Whitespace collapse + `split()` |
| `line.lower()` (E2) | English fertility inflated +2.84%; hin/eng ratio understated (5.89 vs 6.06). Hindi unaffected | Preserve casing |
| Baseline = `langs[0]` (E5) | Ratio and "worse/better" verdict flip with CLI arg order; underlying numbers invariant | `--baseline` flag + reciprocity test |
| Random seed (obs.) | No-op — no random functions involved | — |

- All v0 figures (eng 1.27, hin 7.45) incorporated the first two bugs.
- The corrected pipeline neutralizes E1/E2 before measurement (confirmed: 0% delta on preprocessed FLORES, MASSIVE).

## What the methodology experiments showed

- **E3 — macro vs micro:** diverge by ≤ 2.06%, direction not consistent; TPS identical under both by construction. Macro for sentence-level questions, micro for budgets.
- **E4 — denominators:** the same Hindi text measures 0.46×–10.65× English depending on denominator and tokenizer. Cross-lingual cost must be tokens per parallel segment (tok/seg); tok/cp is within-language only.
- **E7 — robustness check:** v0's "two metrics agree (5.89× / 7.0×), so it's robust" was invalid — those are two ratios of the same token count. On identical text the five denominators span 2.82×–11.11×.

## What actually drives the numbers

- **E6 — tokenizer, not script:** Hindi/English fertility goes 5.89× (gpt2) → 1.00× (muril, sarvam-1); Dravidian 16–21× → 1.7–2.8 tok/w (~90% collapse). English even has *more* chars/word than Hindi (5.97 vs 5.10), refuting v0's root cause directly.
- **E7 — routing flips:** per sentence, Hindi = 4.82× English on gpt2 but **0.84× on muril**. "Budget 6× for Hindi" is a property of gpt2, not Hindi.
- Deploying an Indic model makes Hindi cost 1.0× English on it; budgeting 6× on top double-counts → **~500–600% over-provisioning**.

## Net effect on REPORT_v0

- Every number in its sample table changes under the corrected pipeline (eng 1.27 → 1.23, tok/char 0.226 → 0.2176; hin moves ~1.8%).
- Its robustness check was never valid.
- Its central recommendation (budget ~6× for Hindi) is double-counted if an Indic model is deployed.
