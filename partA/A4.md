# Recommendation Memo: Tokenizer and Routing for Indic Languages

## Recommendation

Do not route all Indic traffic to a separate stack on a flat 6× premise. Decide routing and capacity per language, using tokens per parallel sentence (tok/seg) measured on the tokenizer of the model that serves the traffic, and move a language to an Indic-aware tokenizer path only when its volume × cost exceeds the fixed cost of that path.

## Corrected headline numbers (Hindi ÷ English, same 10-line sample, corrected pipeline)

| Denominator | `gpt2` | Indic-aware (`muril`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tokens per word (v0 said 5.89×) | 6.16× | 1.07× |
| **Tokens per aligned sentence (the decision number)** | **4.82×** | **0.84×** |
| Tokens per codepoint (v0 said 7.0×) | 7.46× | 1.29× |
| Tokens per grapheme | 11.11× | 1.92× |
| Tokens per byte | 2.82× | 0.49× |

- At scale (FLORES-200, 997 parallel sentences, tokens per word ÷ English): Hindi is 6.10× on `gpt2` and 0.96–0.97× on Indic-aware tokenizers. Telugu, Kannada, Tamil and Malayalam are 16.07×, 17.86×, 19.38× and 21.39× on `gpt2`, and 1.35–1.94× on Indic-aware tokenizers.
- Tokens per sentence is the decision number because parallel sentences hold meaning constant across languages, while words, characters, graphemes and bytes do not. v0's 5.89× and 7.0× were two ratios of one token count; on identical text the five denominators span 2.82× to 11.11×.
- v0's root cause is refuted: Hindi has fewer characters per word than English (5.10 vs 5.97), and the penalty disappears under Indic-aware tokenizers.

## Routing recommendation

1. **Capacity:** replace the flat 6× with a per-language multiplier equal to the mean tok/seg ratio against English on the serving tokenizer, and size headroom from the p95. On the audited `gpt2` tokenizer, Hindi is 4.82×.
2. **Routing:** for each language L, move it to an Indic-aware path only if C(L) × (1 − r\_new ÷ r\_old) exceeds the amortized fixed and quality-risk cost of that path, where C(L) is monthly token spend, r\_old the current tok/seg ratio and r\_new the candidate path's. The Dravidian languages have the largest penalty, so they clear this threshold at the lowest volume, then Hindi.
3. **Otherwise stay and budget at the measured ratio.** Budgeting 6× on top of a dedicated Indic path double-counts the penalty and over-provisions by roughly 500%.
4. **Inputs leadership must supply:** monthly token volume per language, price per token, and the fixed cost of a second path.

## Biggest caveat

Every figure measures tokenization on clean, translated text (a 10-line sample and FLORES-200), not serving cost, latency, answer quality or live traffic, which is often code-mixed or romanized. A tokenizer cannot change without its model, so the quality of any alternative path is untested here, and the Dravidian per-sentence ratios must be measured before their capacity multipliers are set; the per-word figures above stand in until then.

## Metric to monitor in production

**Per-language p95 of the paired per-sentence token ratio (tokens in language L ÷ tokens in English for the same sentence), on a parallel canary set replayed weekly.** Pairing removes sentence-length effects, and a per-language p95 catches a tail regression in one language that a pooled Indic average would hide. Alert when any language's p95 exceeds its FLORES-200 baseline on the serving tokenizer by more than 25%.
