# Recommendation Memo: Tokenizer and Routing for Indic Languages

## Recommendation

Do not route all Indic traffic to a separate stack on a flat 6× premise. Decide routing and capacity per language, using tokens per parallel sentence (tok/seg) measured on the tokenizer of the model that serves the traffic, and move a language to an Indic-aware model/stack path only when its volume × cost exceeds the fixed cost of that path.

## Corrected headline numbers (FLORES-200, 997 parallel sentences; Hindi ÷ English)

| Denominator | `gpt2` | Indic-aware (`muril`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tokens per word (v0 said 5.89× on the 10-line sample) | 6.35× | 0.99× |
| **Tokens per parallel sentence (the decision number)** | **7.45×** | **1.16×** |
| Tokens per codepoint (v0 said 7.0× on the 10-line sample) | 7.43× | 1.16× |
| Tokens per grapheme | 10.72× | 1.67× |
| Tokens per byte | 2.89× | 0.45× |

- The 10-line sample is a smoke test: 4.82× per sentence on `gpt2` versus 7.45× on FLORES (`A3.md` §8), too small for cross-language ratios.
- Dravidian per-sentence ratios: Telugu 13.03×, Kannada 13.56×, Tamil 15.43×, Malayalam 15.13× on `gpt2`; 1.22×, 1.06×, 1.05×, 1.17× on `muril`. Per-word ratios are not used: the word is not a constant cross-script unit.
- Tokens per sentence is the decision number: parallel sentences hold meaning constant; words, characters, graphemes and bytes do not. v0's 5.89× and 7.0× were two ratios of one token count; the five denominators span 2.82×–11.11× (sample) and 2.89×–10.72× (FLORES).
- v0's root cause is refuted: Hindi has fewer characters per word than English (5.10 vs 5.97), and the per-sentence penalty collapses from 7.45× to 1.16× under an Indic-aware tokenizer.

## Routing recommendation

1. **Capacity:** replace the flat 6× with a per-language multiplier equal to the mean tok/seg ratio against English on the serving tokenizer, and size headroom from the p95. On `gpt2`, Hindi is 7.45× (FLORES); under `muril`, 1.16×.
2. **Routing:** for each language L, move it to an Indic-aware model path only if C(L) × (1 − r\_new ÷ r\_old) exceeds the amortized fixed and quality-risk cost of that path, where C(L) is monthly spend in currency, r\_old the current tok/seg ratio and r\_new the candidate path's. Dravidian has the largest penalty, so it clears at the lowest volume, then Hindi.
3. **Otherwise stay and budget at the measured ratio.** Budgeting 6× on top of a dedicated Indic path double-counts the penalty and over-provisions by roughly 400–500% (6× versus 1.0–1.16× needed).
4. **Inputs leadership must supply:** monthly token volume per language, price per token, and the fixed cost of a second path.

## Biggest caveat

Every figure measures tokenization on clean, translated text (FLORES-200 and a 10-line smoke test), not serving cost, latency, answer quality or live traffic, which is often code-mixed or romanized. Dravidian per-sentence ratios are measured on FLORES but must be re-measured on the serving tokenizer and live traffic before capacity is locked. A tokenizer cannot change without its model, so any alternative path's quality is untested here.

## Metric to monitor in production

**Per-language p95 of the paired per-sentence token ratio (tokens in language L ÷ tokens in English for the same sentence), on a parallel canary set sampled from live traffic and replayed weekly.** Pairing removes sentence-length effects; a per-language p95 catches a tail regression that a pooled Indic average would hide. Alert when any language's p95 exceeds its FLORES-200 baseline on the serving tokenizer by more than 25%.
